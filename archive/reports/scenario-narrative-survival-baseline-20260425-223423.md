## Section A — Run Identification & Authored Intent

### Authored intent

> Survival baseline scenario for Worldwake.
>
> Purpose: prove that agents with only survival-relevant scenario overrides can bootstrap food/water access through the normal perception and exploration path over a full 1440-tick observer run.

### Topology

The run takes place on a four-place graph. Riverside Camp (a camp with a latrine, a well, and a washbasin) sits three travel ticks from Fertile Fields (an orchard-row farm), four travel ticks from Forest Clearing (a forest containing a second well and washbasin), and six travel ticks from Hillside Shelter (a sibling camp with a latrine but no harvestable resources). Forest Clearing connects to Fertile Fields at two travel ticks and to Hillside Shelter at five. All edges are bidirectional. There is no concealment, no contention policy, and no force-claim or jurisdictional substrate authored on any place.

The world holds three resource sources: water at Camp Well in Riverside Camp (capacity twenty units, regenerating one unit every three ticks), water at Forest Well in Forest Clearing (same shape), and apples at North Orchard in Fertile Fields (capacity twenty-four, regenerating one unit every two ticks). Hillside Shelter has nothing harvestable.

### Cast

Three AI-controlled agents share an authored intent of bare survival. Agent A and Agent B both begin co-located at Riverside Camp; Agent C begins alone at Forest Clearing. None are presented as principals over the others — the survival-health contract does not single any of them out, and all three carry the same eight-row profile shape (utility, perception, tell, cognitive, execution-budget, drive-thresholds, metabolism, exploration). For the purposes of this report all three are treated as tracked principals.

### Survival-health contract

The contract authored on this scenario forbids any agent from running a need above its critical threshold for more than 100 consecutive ticks, forbids more than twenty consecutive idle ticks while any need is elevated above 300 permille, and requires every agent to exercise eat, drink, sleep, relieve, and wash at least once during the run. There are no per-need overrides; every drive is governed by the per-agent thresholds in the AgentDef.

### What the run is meant to demonstrate

The intended demonstration is causal-substrate sufficiency: a flat survival profile, a sparse facility map, and zero seeded beliefs about non-starting places should produce three agents who discover the world by walking it, perceive resource sources by being near them, and bootstrap a self-care loop without scripting. This is the foundational scenario in the project's roadmap; later scenarios layer travel physiology, drive escalation, contested resources, social transfer, trade, justice, combat, and institutional features on top of the substrate exercised here.

## Section B — Gameplay Mechanics Exercised

This run activated five gameplay-feature rows from the project's Feature Catalog: the five basic needs collectively (Eat, Drink, Sleep, Relieve, Wash), need-driven exploration, and activation-decay perception. No further rows fired. The substrate for facility-queue contention was present in the affordance set but agents resolved conflicts through reservation-blocker replanning rather than committing `queue_for_facility_use`. The remaining roadmap rows — travel physiology, drive escalation as an authored feature, all economic and institutional and conflict rows — are deliberately inactive on this baseline scenario and are listed at the end.

**Basic needs (Eat).** Hunger is regulated by the metabolism profile's hunger rate and a per-agent threshold ladder, and is satisfied through the two-step path of harvesting an apple at North Orchard (recipe `Harvest Apples`) followed by an `eat` commit. Agent A carries hunger weight 700 with metabolism rate two; Agent B carries the run's highest hunger weight 750 with the highest hunger rate three; Agent C carries hunger weight 700 with rate two. All three drive thresholds put critical hunger near 820–850 permille. Across the run, Agent A committed `eat` twenty-four times and `harvest:Harvest Apples` twelve times; Agent B matched at twenty-four eats and twelve harvests; Agent C, despite starting farthest from the orchard, ate the most — thirty-three commits and seventeen harvests. Hunger maxima never exceeded 503 permille (Agent B's peak), comfortably below every agent's critical threshold, and ticks-above-750 was zero for every agent.

**Basic needs (Drink).** Thirst is the run's most pressing initial drive: all three agents start above 200 permille of authored thirst and the planner's first goal commit on tick 0 is `AcquireCommodity { Water }` for every agent. Agent C (thirst weight 725, the run's highest, and thirst rate four) drank ten times and harvested water five times, all at Forest Well. Agent A drank seven times and harvested four; Agent B drank eight times and harvested four. The two camps' wells were never exhausted — peak thirst across the run was 309 permille (Agent B), again far below the 800–820 critical floor.

**Basic needs (Sleep).** Sleep is satisfied without travel by committing `sleep` at the agent's current place. The fatigue weight is 650 across all agents and the metabolism profile fixes a rest efficiency of twenty permille per tick of sleep. Sleep dominated every agent's tick budget: Agent A committed sleep 143 times, Agent B 144 times, Agent C 146 times — together 433 of the 1440 ticks were spent in committed sleeps, distributed in chunks of roughly ten to twelve per 100-tick bin throughout the run. Fatigue stayed in the 117–344 permille band on every agent; nobody approached the 900 critical line. The only mechanically-flagged anomaly in the run is a length-two `sleep → sleep` loop on Agent A repeated three times, which is an artifact of consecutive sleep commits resolving back-to-back rather than a behavioral defect.

**Basic needs (Relieve).** Relief is satisfied either by `toilet` at a latrine-tagged place or by `relieve_wilderness` outside one. Bladder weight is 650, bladder rate four per tick. Agent A committed `relieve_wilderness` fourteen times and `toilet` nine times; Agent B fourteen wildernesses and six toilets; Agent C twenty-six wildernesses and zero toilets. The wilderness-relief preference reflects the location-history facts: Agent C spent 969 of 1440 ticks at Fertile Fields, which carries no Latrine tag, so `toilet` was structurally unavailable to her at her dominant place. The bladder accident-tolerance line (forty ticks) was never breached on any agent; bladder peaked at 540 permille on Agent B.

**Basic needs (Wash).** Wash is satisfied by `wash` at a WashBasin-tagged facility. Agents A and B each committed `wash` four times across the 1440-tick run; Agent C also four. Wash commits cluster around 200, 500, 900, and 1200 in the 100-tick bin tables, each cycle aligning with dirtiness rising back into a few hundred permille. Dirtiness peaked at 369 permille (Agent B); the 920-permille critical line was never approached.

**Need-driven exploration.** The exploration profile makes every agent emit `ExploreLocation` candidates whenever a need crosses the profile's `need_activation_threshold` (325 for Agent A, 275 for Agent B, 300 for Agent C) and a non-starting place exists in their landmark depth. The profiles also award an `exploration_arrival_boost` (650, 700, 675 respectively) on first arrival at a discovered place. The mechanic was decisive in the opening minutes of the run: at tick 1 Agent B's `AcquireCommodity { Water }` plan was blocked at Camp Well by a reservation conflict with Agent A (who had committed `harvest:Harvest Water` on tick 0), the planner suppressed the blocked goal, the exploration emitter offered `ExploreLocation { target_place: Fertile Fields, motivating_need: NeedDriven(Hunger) }`, the same-class margin chose it, and Agent B set off toward Fertile Fields, arriving at tick 4. Agent A followed an analogous trajectory three ticks later (`ExploreLocation` plan adopted at tick 3, arriving at Fertile Fields by tick 6), abandoning the explore goal in favor of a freshly-feasible `AcquireCommodity { Apple }` once the orchard was perceived. Agent C, who already had a water source at her starting Forest Clearing, did not need to bootstrap her food path through exploration — the orchard at Fertile Fields was within her two-tick frontier and she committed travel to it directly under hunger pressure.

**Activation-decay perception.** The perception profile gates which entities feed into an agent's belief store on each observation tick (entity activation threshold 100, claim confidence threshold fifty, observation buffer twelve, fidelity 900) and allows a need-salience boost (500 for Agent A, 600 for Agent B, 550 for Agent C) above an urgency threshold near 425–450 permille. The mechanic produced 170 observations on Agent A (154 passing the gate) reaching 41 unique entities; 177 observations on Agent B (154 passing) reaching 46 unique entities; 189 observations on Agent C (163 passing) reaching 55 unique entities. The fidelity-and-salience configuration is what lets the planner reason about the orchard, the wells, and the lots of harvested apples sitting in the Fertile Fields ground — all entities that had to be discovered through perception rather than seeded into beliefs. Confidence policy parameters (direct-observation base 900, staleness penalty two per tick, report base 700, rumor base 400) are authored but only the direct-observation branch saw use in this run because there are no peer-information or institutional-memory channels active.

## Section D — Cross-Agent and Emergent Phenomena

The only cross-agent phenomenon worth narrating in this run is the **shared-resource contention episode at Camp Well and North Orchard**. Agents A and B start the run within reach of the same Camp Well water lot at tick 0; Agent A wins the start race by reserving the lot, and Agent B's same-tick `AcquireCommodity { Water }` plan is rejected at action start with a `BlockingFact(ReservationConflict)` expiring twenty ticks later. The planner does the right thing here without contention machinery: it suppresses the blocked goal, reads the exploration emitter's `ExploreLocation { Fertile Fields }` candidate as an alternative drive on hunger pressure, switches goals on the same-class margin, and Agent B departs for Fertile Fields immediately. The rerouting transforms a contention loss into a beneficial early discovery: Fertile Fields is the only apple-source in the world, and Agent B becomes the first arrival there at tick 4. Agent A then experiences the symmetric event at tick 7, when her own arrival at the orchard collides with Agent B's reservation on the apple lot — the same blocker pattern resolves the same way, and she falls back to consuming her in-progress water.

This episode is the **only** evidence in the run for the contention substrate's behavior, and even then it never lit up the `queue_for_facility_use` action — the agents resolved the contention through their own goal-switch margins, not through queueing for a grant. Five further blocker events at the orchard at ticks 65, 66, 408, and 1085 followed the same pattern. None of them triggered exploration of Hillside Shelter or Forest Clearing for additional apple sources because none exist in this scenario.

No other cross-agent phenomena fired this run. There were no witness chains (no `tell` commits, no perception-bridged belief acquisitions), no trade exchanges, no social transfers, no hostile encounters, no escort handoffs, no posse formation. Every multi-agent moment in the run was either co-location-by-coincidence (Agents A, B, and C all ended up at Fertile Fields by tick 6 and remained there for hundreds of ticks) or co-location-by-resource-attractor.

## Section E — Realism, Resourcefulness, Resilience

### Realism

Three moments demonstrate genuine constraint propagation in this run. First, at tick 1 Agent B's blocked water plan does not become an indefinite wait or a safe but pointless replan — it propagates through the goal-suppression machinery into an exploration drive that depends on hunger pressure being already-elevated. The same agent who could not get water at the well becomes the agent who founds the food path for the cast, and she does so under a concrete motivating-need rather than a generic "look around" impulse. Second, at tick 5 Agent C's `Relieve` commit selects `relieve_wilderness` rather than `toilet` because Forest Clearing carries no Latrine tag — a place-tag fact propagating into action-affordance generation rather than being checked at execution time. Third, the planner's repeated `MotiveScore Sleep@none > AcquireCommodity { Apple }` overrides on Agents A and B during the 600–800 tick window reflect fatigue pressure (around 300 permille × weight 650 = 195,000) genuinely outscoring an in-progress acquisition (apple hunger pressure × weight 700, in the 100,000–170,000 band when hunger sits in the low-200s) — sleep wins on its score, not on a hard interrupt rule.

### Resourcefulness

Three moments show agents reaching goals through more than the most direct path. First, Agent B's tick-1 substitution of an unknown-place exploration for a contended-resource acquisition is the run's clearest resourcefulness signal: rather than queueing or waiting out the blocker at Camp Well, she invests two travel ticks plus a first-arrival exploration boost (700) into discovering Fertile Fields, and the investment pays off across the rest of the run. Second, Agent C's bidirectional life between Forest Clearing (water source) and Fertile Fields (food source) — eleven travels in 1440 ticks — is the planner solving a two-resource problem with a two-place commute rather than dragging both consumables to one place; the same-class margin between `AcquireCommodity { Water }@Forest Clearing` and `AcquireCommodity { Apple }@Fertile Fields` keeps re-pivoting on whichever pressure wins the weight×pressure comparison. Third, Agent A's tick-1010 plan for `AcquireCommodity { Apple }` at Fertile Fields — selected with a `Travel → MoveCargo` two-step plan and pruning notes that show the planner kept Forest Clearing in the candidate set but pruned it on cost — demonstrates the planner's willingness to consider further-afield apple sources before committing to the closer one.

### Resilience

Three moments show recovery from setbacks without collapse. First, every reservation-conflict blocker in the run (twenty plus two on Agent B, twenty on Agent A, none on Agent C) cleared inside the cognitive profile's `transient_block_ticks` envelope of twenty ticks; the planner did not escalate to `structural_block_ticks` (200), did not deepen-search itself into budget exhaustion (zero budget-exhaustion events recorded across all three agents), and did not abandon the goal class altogether — it waited, replanned, and tried again. Second, Agent A's anomaly-flagged length-two `sleep → sleep` loop at the end of the run is a benign artifact of consecutive sleeps resolving in the same decision tick rather than a stuck-loop pathology — her behavioral-transition narrowing at tick 1400 (six action types to one) coincides with all five of her needs sitting below 300 permille, exactly the situation where sleep should dominate. Third, the survival-health contract terminating the run at tick 1440 finds zero ticks above 750 permille for any need on any agent, zero authored-critical windows entered, and all five required self-care families exercised — a flat survival profile in a sparse world produced 1440 ticks of homeostatic equilibrium under a resource regime where two of the three agents were drinking from the same well and all three were eating from the same orchard.
