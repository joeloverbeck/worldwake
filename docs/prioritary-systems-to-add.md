# Prioritary Systems to Add

Bluntly: you are not blocked on “smarter AI.” You are blocked on missing world nouns. The goldens already prove a serious causal spine: 19 golden files, 263 tests, 110 annotated scenario blocks, plus deterministic replay, save/load continuity, and “world runs without observers.” The proved chains already include road predation, bandit camp destruction and reconstitution, theft → accusation → punishment, rumor-led wasted trips, office vacancy → patrol gap → crime, supply disruption → route reroute, and lots of interruption/replanning behavior.

So the next leap is not more planner cleverness. The next leap is from “agents can react causally” to “the world can mint lawful adventure hooks, destinations, and aftermath.” In the design note’s situation-class terms, you are already pretty strong on dangerous roads, outlawry, theft/investigation, stale information, interrupted errands, patrol breakdown, shortages, and contention. The conspicuous holes are predator displacement, bounties/notices/contracts/warrants, search/rescue/hunt, persistent sites with changing occupancy, boundary shocks, broad social obligation chains, and long-horizon settlement change. FOUNDATIONS makes that especially clear by elevating the beast → caravan attack → report → bounty → hunt → reward chain to a canonical regression target.

This ordering is relative to your current proven base, not a greenfield rewrite.

## 1. Social artifact and adjudication layer

This is the single highest-leverage missing system. The design note explicitly frames bounties, notices, contracts, and warrants as the lawful replacement for quest pipelines, and FOUNDATIONS says those artifacts must be first-class world state rather than UI abstractions or hidden controller logic. Right now your goldens prove that the world can generate incidents, reports, accusations, punishments, office decisions, and trade, but the inventory has no dedicated bounty/notice/contract/warrant suite. That means incidents can happen without becoming socially legible calls to action.

Build this as a generalized public-record / claim / adjudication substrate, not as a BountySystem one-off. A bounty should be a real record with issuer, authority basis, jurisdiction, sponsor or treasury, proof requirements, reward quantity, posting place, expiry, claimant state, resolution history, and copies or retellings. The same substrate should cover notices, warrants, contracts, accusations, payment claims, and later letters and decrees. Scenario 73 already proves that record consultation is a lawful carrier of political knowledge; generalize that idea from office registers into public opportunity artifacts.

Your first goldens here should be: raid reported → bounty posted at a real place; two claimants compete for one reward; false proof rejected; stale notice expires; treasury empty prevents payout without cheating; jurisdiction mismatch blocks issuance or payment. If this layer is right, existing bandit raids, theft chains, patrol failures, and future beast attacks instantly become discoverable content without a quest manager.

## 2. Nonhuman ecology: territory, lairs, displacement, roaming threats

Right now human predation is real. Monster ecology is not. The design note singles out predator displacement as a core situation class, and FOUNDATIONS makes the beast-starvation-to-bounty chain canonical. Your current goldens prove bandit predation, pursuit, camp destruction, diaspora, and reconstitution, but there is no equivalent substrate for beasts, predators, or other nonhuman roaming threats. Don’t reskin bandits and call it done.

What you need is a real ecology slice: habitat preference, territory or range, lair/site attachment, prey classes, hunger pressure, injury and fear state, territorial conflict, carrion use, and displacement triggers. The point is not “spawn scary creature on road.” The point is “local prey depletion, competition, injury, or human pressure made this creature’s movement lawful.” Lairs should be real places; kills should leave real aftermath; sightings should travel by the same belief/report paths you already use elsewhere.

The first proving scenarios should be: prey shortage displaces beast from old territory onto a trade road; injured beast retreats and changes its aggression pattern; two predators compete and the loser invades settled land; local livestock losses create rumor and fear before the town has certainty. This is the missing half of your “travel itself is adventure” dream.

## 3. Search, tracking, hunting, and rescue

Your pursuit suite proves witness-based chase to a last-known place, stale arrival failure, and re-pursuit after flight. Your investigation suite proves expectation mismatch, missing-entity discovery, accusation chains, and patrol-driven discovery. What you do not yet prove is generalized clue-based search: following tracks, canvassing for fresh reports, searching likely sites, recovering proof, or conducting rescue under uncertainty. That gap is exactly why the beast-bounty chain still isn’t there.

This system should add physical clue carriers and search intent, not omniscient target acquisition. Tracks, spoor, drag marks, blood, broken brush, recent kill sites, camp remnants, and “last seen heading east” reports should all exist as evidence with freshness, provenance, and decay. Searchers should reason over search areas, likely destinations, and stale/conflicting clues. Rescue and hunt are the same substrate with different completion conditions.

The proving goldens should be: hunter follows fresh spoor to a lair; stale tracks waste an expedition; missing traveler search finds a corpse instead of a living victim; two searchers split on conflicting leads; proof-of-kill is accepted only if it matches the posted terms. Without this, a bounty layer will collapse into magical target knowledge, which would violate the whole project.

## 4. Group commitments and logistics: caravans, escorts, expeditions, parties

Your trade and supply-chain goldens already prove merchant restock, cargo movement, seller identity, bandit interception, and route adaptation. But they are still mostly individual or thinly institutional logistics. Your exemplar chain needs caravans, survivors, authorities, mercenaries, and hunters who can actually band together. That means you need an explicit group-commitment substrate.

Do not build a magic “party AI blob.” Build group records: membership, leader, itinerary, rendezvous points, cargo manifest, escort obligations, role assignments, share rules, and abandonment or split conditions. Caravans, patrol detachments, escorts, hunting bands, and rescue parties should all be different uses of the same substrate. Individual agents should still plan as individuals, but against concrete group commitments and obligations.

The first goldens should be: caravan ambush leaves wounded survivors and scattered cargo; one survivor reports while another limps elsewhere; mercenaries accept a hunt contract and split payment; escort deserts when unpaid or badly wounded; expedition dissolves after proof is recovered. This is how roads stop being “single-agent travel paths” and become social arteries.

## 5. Persistent sites with layered occupancy and history

Your topology is already decent. The coverage matrix shows lots of named places and multi-leg routes, and the scenario map proves meaningful movement through them. But most current places are still functional nodes. The design note wants something richer: ruins, caves, shrines, watchtowers, mines, manors, warehouses, and lairs as places with identity, access points, containers, traces, changing occupants, and layered history. That is still weak.

This should be a general site substrate. A lair is a site. A bandit hideout is a site. A half-abandoned mine is a site. A manor with sealed rooms and ledgers is a site. Occupancy should change through ordinary world action: squatters, smugglers, beasts, fugitives, officials, pilgrims, scavengers. The site should preserve traces of former occupancy so that later visitors can reason from evidence rather than from designer-authored lore drops.

The first goldens should be: abandoned watchtower becomes smuggler stash, then beast lair, then militia outpost; site clearing leaves containers and traces; stale rumor sends hunters to a site whose occupants already changed. This is the system that will make “there is a place out there worth going to” lawful instead of authored.

## 6. Material institutions: treasuries, budgets, assets, delegated duties

You already have offices, succession, force control, political knowledge locality, and patrol behavior. What you do not yet have strongly enough is institutional materiality. In other words: the town can currently know and decide more than it can materially own, pay, store, or delegate. That is the next gap. FOUNDATIONS is explicit that institutions must act through offices, records, rules, places, assets, and budgets, not singleton manager logic.

Add real treasuries, earmarks, payrolls, office inventories, delegated duty assignments, sponsor assets, and release procedures. A bounty should not be “reward promised”; it should be “reward backed by this treasury or sponsor chest.” Guards should not just patrol because the role says so; they should also be downstream of budgets, duty rosters, and substitution when offices fail. This system will make existing office/patrol scenarios deeper without changing their causal nature.

The first goldens should be: bounty posted but unpaid because the treasury is empty; unpaid guards reduce patrol coverage; office vacancy delays budget release; a merchant sponsor funds a hunt the town cannot afford. This is where institutional consequence stops being purely social and becomes materially legible.

## 7. Boundary processes and remote regions

The design note and FOUNDATIONS both push hard on this. Current shortages and route dangers are mostly intra-map. You already prove route adaptation and supply disruption inside the simulated topology, but the world still needs lawful outside causes: upstream shortages, bridge collapse beyond the core map, migrating herds, refugee inflow, external decrees, delayed convoys, outside war, disease import, and so on.

Build explicit boundary channels: source region, route or transmission medium, delay, capacity, observables, and failure modes. Off-map is not nowhere. It must be either lower-fidelity same-world state or a declared external process with provenance and delay. This system is the difference between a map that reacts only to itself and a world that feels larger than the loaded region.

The first goldens should be: external bridge collapse delays a convoy and causes a local shortage; outside herd migration pushes predators inward; remote political decree changes border inspections with actual delay; refugee arrival strains food stocks and shelter. This will also make settlement change and predator movement much more believable.

## 8. Settlement lifecycle: rise, decline, abandonment, reuse

This is later than the items above because it should emerge from them, not replace them. The design note wants settlements to prosper, hollow out, become dangerous, or be abandoned through ordinary pressures. That cannot be a settlement_health score. It has to be the aggregate result of population, role coverage, traffic, local production, security, institutional performance, dependency chains, and property transfer.

Once you have material institutions, boundary shocks, caravans, predator ecology, and persistent sites, settlement lifecycle becomes tractable. Households leave because roads fail, food fails, offices stay vacant, payroll stops, or trade shifts elsewhere. New ruins appear because places were actually vacated. New enclaves appear because sites were actually reused. That is exactly the kind of long-horizon consequence the note is after.

The first goldens should be: patrol breakdown and shortage drive merchant departure; abandoned houses become squat sites; restored convoy traffic and office succession reverse decline; lair pressure hollows out a hamlet and creates a future delve site. This is the system that lets the world author its own future content.

## 9. Social obligations: debt, kinship, grudges, revenge, protection

This is lower than the others because it enriches motives rather than creating whole new causal classes, but it still matters. The design note explicitly calls out grudges, loyalties, and revenge chains, and right now those are only partially represented through loyalty and courage in political or coercive scenarios. You need broader social obligation memory to make agents feel human rather than purely opportunistic.

Do not model this as a single relationship score. Model obligations and remembered harms as records: debt owed, kin relation, unpaid contract, protected dependent, insult, betrayal, murder of a relation, failed justice, favor received. Those records should feed ordinary reasoning and social artifact issuance. An unpaid bounty can become a debt. A murder can become a revenge chain if the institution fails. A rescued victim can create loyalty or obligation.

The first goldens should be: unpaid reward creates a grudge against the issuer; murdered caravaner’s kin pursue revenge after weak official response; protection obligation causes a guard or mercenary to accept bad odds. This is the step that gives you memorable human spite, not just systemic motion.

One architectural caution: I am not recommending nine isolated managers. Broadly, these should collapse into a few reusable substrates. Bounties, notices, warrants, accusations, and contracts belong in one social-artifact/adjudication substrate. Caravans, escorts, patrol detachments, and hunting parties belong in one group-commitment substrate. Lairs, ruins, hideouts, and manors belong in one site substrate. Keeping them broad is what will keep you aligned with the anti-workaround mandate in FOUNDATIONS.

If I were driving the roadmap, I would make one flagship acceptance suite next: golden_beast_bounty_chain.rs. It should prove, end to end, that territory depletion or competition displaces a predator; the predator attacks a caravan on a real route; survivors report through actual travel; an office-holder posts a bounty at a real place from a real treasury; hunters read the notice and form an expedition; search can fail on stale clues or succeed on fresh ones; proof is adjudicated; reward is paid once, or lawfully denied if the proof is false or the treasury is insolvent. If that suite passes without any quest-script shortcut, you will have crossed from “impressive causal simulation” into “credible emergent RPG substrate.”

What I would not prioritize next: more abstract danger/scarcity scores, another generic AI rewrite, encounter directors, quest managers, or decorative realism like weather/chemistry/deep crafting unless they obviously add new carriers of consequence. The docs are brutally clear about that, and your current goldens already show the planner/locality/determinism spine is good enough to carry richer nouns instead of smarter heuristics.

In one line: move from incident generation to lawful opportunity generation. That is the shortest path from your current system to the actual Bethesda/DF/RimWorld dream.
